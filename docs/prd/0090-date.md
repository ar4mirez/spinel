# PRD 0090 — `date`

Issue: [#60](https://github.com/ar4mirez/spinel/issues/60) · Phase 3 ·
`area:gems`

## Objective

`require "date"` gives `Date` and `DateTime` as the `date` gem 3.5.1 that
CRuby 4.0.6 ships: making them in every calendar form, arithmetic, `strftime`,
`Date._parse` and `parse`, `strptime`, and the ISO 8601, RFC 3339, XML
Schema, RFC 2822, HTTP and JIS X 0301 formats both ways. `library/date` and
`library/datetime` were blocked, and with them `Time#to_date`, `time.rb`,
Rails' dates and every log parser.

## Design

**The gem's two halves.** `lib/date.rb` is the gem's own Ruby half,
unchanged: `Date::VERSION`, `Date#infinite?`, `Date::Infinity`. It requires
`date_core`, which in CRuby is the C extension — `date_core.c`,
`date_parse.c`, `date_strftime.c`, `date_strptime.c` — and here is
`lib/date_core.rb`, those in Ruby.

**Kept as CRuby keeps it.** A date is its chronological Julian day, the
seconds into it and the nanoseconds past those, all in UTC, beside the offset
and the day of calendar reform; the civil fields are worked out in local time
when asked for, so `inspect` and `marshal_dump` say what CRuby's say,
nanoseconds kept as a Rational when there is a fraction of one. The calendar
conversions are `date_core.c`'s floating-point formulas turned into integer
division — `floor(365.25 * y)` is `(1461 * y).div(4)` — so a year far from now
is as exact as one near it.

**Parsing is `date_parse.c`'s, in its order.** `_parse` blanks out what each
of its parsers matches — the day name, the time and zone, then European,
American, ISO, JIS, VMS, slashed and dotted dates, ISO weeks and ordinals,
an apostrophed year, a month name, an ordinal day, a run of digits — and
`s3e` decides which of three numbers is the year. The strict formats are
their own anchored patterns. Missing fields are completed from today the way
`rt_complete_frags` completes them. `strptime` reads each directive as
`date_strptime.c` does, its width bounded only when a number follows.

Every one was checked against ruby 4.0.6 on a corpus of inputs — 160
`strftime`, `_parse` and arithmetic cases, 70 for the strict formats, 80 for
`strptime` — until the two printed the same.

**`# shareable_constant_value:`**, which `time.rb` carries, is compiled now:
the value of a constant written under it is deep-frozen when the mode says so
— a literal under `literal`, everything under the experimental two.
`ponytail:` a non-literal value under `literal` is not checked for being
shareable, which waits on Ractors (#117).

`Float#<=>` gained CRuby's rule for an infinity beside a Numeric that says
whether it is infinite, which `Date::JULIAN <=> Date::Infinity.new` needs.

## Results

`library/date`, `library/datetime` and `library/time` from blocked to
**554** of 554 passing.

Corpus 25,874 to **26,355** passing, 0 failed: compared example by example
with `main`, 461 blocked examples pass, and none that passed fails. Every
pass agrees with ruby 4.0.6.
