# PRD 0092 — `Integer`: conversions, coercion and the shifts that leave the word

Issue: [#17](https://github.com/ar4mirez/spinel/issues/17) · Phase 2 ·
`area:core-lib`

## Objective

[PRD 0037](0037-integer-the-half-that-is-not-the-harness.md) left `core/integer`
waiting on #145. Now that mspec runs on Spinel, most of what blocked it turned
out to be `Integer`'s own: 85 blocked and 11 tagged examples out of 598, at 502
passing.

`scripts/spec.sh core/integer`: 502 passed, 85 blocked, 11 skipped. The blocked

## Design

**One Rust change: forwarding a non-Integer operand.** `&`, `|`, `^`, `<<` and
`>>` are primitives. Given an operand that is not an Integer, they used to
raise `no implicit conversion into Integer`. Now they send
`__bits_other__(other, op)`, which is Ruby. The bitwise operators ask the
operand to `coerce` and retry on the pair, provided the first half of the pair
has the operator. If it does not (a Float's `coerce` answers Floats), the error
names the original operand, as `rb_num_coerce_bit` does. The shifts convert with
`to_int` instead. This is `Native::NumOp`'s arrangement for `+`, reached only
after the fast path declines.

**Shifts past the word.** A right shift by a bignum, or by 2^32 or more,
answers the sign. A left shift of zero is zero. A left shift of anything else by
2^32 or more is `RangeError: shift width too big`. ruby/spec allows this limit
for engines other than CRuby (CRuby's own is 2^67,
bugs.ruby-lang.org/issues/18518). Both used to refuse.

**`Integer.__to_int__` is `rb_to_int`**, used by `[]`, the shifts, `digits`,
`round`, `Integer.sqrt` and the `*bits?` methods. A `to_int` that answers a
non-Integer raises with CRuby 4.1's wording, `can't convert X into Integer`
(bugs.ruby-lang.org/issues/21864). ruby/spec's `raise_consistent_error` checks
that wording on every engine except CRuby before 4.1, and #336 made the same
choice for `Data`.

**A private `coerce` is called.** `Numeric#__coerce_pair__` uses
`respond_to?(:coerce, true)` and `__send__`, as CRuby's `rb_check_funcall`
does. `**`, `div`, `fdiv` and `remainder` coerce through it.

**`fdiv` rounds once.** Two Integers that are not both exact as Floats are
divided exactly. The quotient is taken to two bits past the 53 a Float keeps, or
fewer bits for a subnormal, with the remainder settling a tie, and is then built
with `Math.ldexp`. The old shift-both-then-divide rounded twice and lost
`1.fdiv(10**323)`.

**`round`'s mode** is checked whenever a digit count is given, even one that
leaves nothing to round, and ignored when none is. Both measured.

**mspec's fixnum helpers.** `spec/spinel/runner.rb` defines `fixnum_max` and
`fixnum_min` as 2^62 - 1 and -2^62, which is `Value::FIXNUM_MAX` and
`FIXNUM_MIN`, after requiring mspec's own definitions so that the spec helper
cannot redefine them.

Each behaviour was measured on ruby 4.0.7. The `coerce` and shift cases are
rows in `crates/spinel-vm/tests/coerce.txt`, generated by
`scripts/coerce-oracle.rb`.

## Results

`core/integer` **502 → 586** of 598, 0 failed. Compared example by example,
85 newly pass and one that passed no longer does: `chr` with a Shift_JIS
`Encoding.default_internal` and an invalid codepoint used to pass because any
number above 255 raised `RangeError`. It now reaches the Shift_JIS refusal
(#344), which says honestly what Spinel cannot do yet. `core/rational` 155 → 159 and
`core/string` 3518 → 3520 come along with it. Corpus 27200 → **27290** passing,
0 failed. `scripts/verify-passes.rb` re-ran all 4829 passes in `core/integer`,
`core/rational`, `core/string`, `core/float` and `core/numeric` on ruby 4.0.7,
and all agree.

Eight tags are gone: the four shift-`to_int` tags, the three `fdiv` tags and the
`round` tag.

## Not done

- **Nine `chr` examples**, the one above among them, need Shift_JIS, EUC-JP and CESU-8, which #19 refused
  to guess at (PRD 0050): #344.
- **`Integer#===` as an alias of `Integer#==`** stays tagged. Integer has no
  `==` of its own, and one written in Ruby does not return on Spinel because
  the VM's equality sends `==` back for some pairs. It needs a primitive: #345.
- **Two `Integer#coerce` tags** keep their reasons. Both are about converting a
  String or an object through `Float()`, which is #18's and #181's.
